# VULGSHIRE — MASTER CARTOGRAPHIC SPECIFICATION

Create a highly detailed, authentic-looking 2D antique medieval map of VULGSHIRE, a fictional Central European metropolis inspired by Prague.

The map must function as a geographically coherent representation of an actual medieval city.

## 1. GEOGRAPHY — NON-NEGOTIABLE

The defining geographical feature of Vulgshire is THREE major rivers converging into ONE enormous lake.

- Three distinct rivers approach the city from different directions.
- Each river must remain visibly separate until reaching the central convergence.
- All three rivers flow into one enormous body of water called Vulgshire Lake.
- There must be exactly three major incoming rivers.
- There must be NO outgoing river after the lake.
- The lake must be a prominent central geographical feature.
- The city must develop around its shoreline.

The lake should have a naturally irregular shoreline, not a perfectly circular shape.

Small islands, peninsulas, waterfront districts and harbour areas are encouraged where geographically appropriate.

Do not create additional major rivers or additional lakes.

## 2. CITY STRUCTURE

Design a large, densely populated medieval European metropolis.

The city should feature:

- An extensive medieval street network.
- Narrow winding streets and larger main roads.
- Dense residential neighbourhoods.
- Large public squares.
- Gothic churches and cathedrals.
- A dominant royal castle and fortress.
- Stone bridges connecting both sides of waterways.
- Fortified city walls and defensive towers.
- Markets, taverns and merchant buildings.
- Harbours, docks and waterfront promenades.

The city must feel like a functioning medieval capital that developed organically over centuries.

Avoid perfectly symmetrical city planning.

## 3. PROPOSED LANDMARKS

Use the following names as initial geographical concepts:

- Crownkeep — royal fortress.
- Great Cathedral — principal religious landmark.
- Old Town — historic urban centre.
- Merchants' Quarter — commercial district.
- Docks — principal harbour.
- Eastern Bazaar — marketplace.
- Southern District — residential district.
- Western Gates — fortified entrance.
- Silver Gate — eastern entrance.
- Ashenwood Forest — surrounding woodland.
- Greyspire Mountains — distant mountain range.

Place landmarks logically according to their purpose.

The castle should occupy a prominent elevated position.
The cathedral should be a major architectural landmark.
The docks should have direct access to the lake.

These names are provisional and can be changed during world development.

## 4. VISUAL STYLE

The map must resemble an antique European medieval cartographer's work.

Use:

- Weathered parchment.
- Hand-drawn ink illustrations.
- Fine dark-brown outlines.
- Muted sepia and earthy colours.
- Detailed individual building footprints.
- Clearly visible roads and alleyways.
- Medieval cartographic symbols.
- Decorative borders.
- An ornate compass rose.
- A geographical legend.
- A readable distance scale.
- Elegant historical lettering.

Avoid modern typography, modern map symbols, bright saturated colours and contemporary satellite-map styling.

## 5. CAMERA — ABSOLUTE REQUIREMENT

STRICT 90-DEGREE TOP-DOWN ORTHOGRAPHIC VIEW.

The camera must look directly down at the geographical surface.

Every part of the city must be represented from above.

Buildings must be depicted through their rooftops and footprints.

There must be:

- No isometric perspective.
- No angled aerial perspective.
- No horizon.
- No visible building facades.
- No cinematic perspective.
- No 3D-rendered city presentation.

This must look like a genuine 2D historical city map.

## 6. COMPOSITION

Show the complete urban settlement and its surrounding geography.

The entire lake, all three incoming rivers, the main city walls and the surrounding countryside must fit within the composition.

Keep important geographical features distinguishable.

Do not crop major rivers or the lake.

Ensure district labels do not obscure important geographical details.

## 7. TITLE

VULGSHIRE

THE CITY OF THREE RIVERS

The finished image should look like an official historical map commissioned by a medieval royal cartographer.

The final result must be suitable as the geographical reference for a large-scale explorable 3D medieval game world.
